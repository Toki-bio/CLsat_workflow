CLsat Locus Viewer — manual

What this is

A locus-resolved browser for the satellite arrays catalogued across the assemblies of this run. Every array in the catalogue is a segmented MASTER locus — a contiguous CLsat-similar domain, split where the dominant subfamily changes along the array. The viewer shows the raw evidence behind each call, so any statement in the paper can be checked at the level of a single locus.

Quick workflow

  • 1. Select a locus. Click a row in the Locus Table (left), or click an array in the Scaffold Overview. You can also type an ID (e.g. L152) into Find locus and press Go.
  • 2. Zoom in. Mouse-wheel to zoom, drag to pan. Zoom until individual monomers resolve — monomer-level tools need the array to be readable, not the whole scaffold.
  • 3. Get FASTA. Fetches the locus sequence from NCBI E-utilities (nothing runs locally).
  • 4. Analyze Monomers. Splits the fetched sequence into individual monomers.
  • 5. Inspect below. Monomer list → monomer map → MSA → terminal-monomer trim.

Panels

  • Scaffold Overview — every locus on the selected scaffold. Bar height = bitscore.
  • Per-window Bitscores — the selected locus scanned against all four CLsat consensuses, one bar per scan window. This is why a locus got its call: a composite array shows the dominant colour switching part-way along, and a contested locus shows two colours running neck-and-neck.
  • Locus Table — filter by ID, assembly, family or flag; the Show and Subtype filters stay in sync with the overview.
  • Locus Ends & Flanks — the unique sequence flanking the array, its truncation status, and the cross-assembly flank matches that define positional orthology.

Monomer analysis

How it is computed: everything under “Analyze Monomers” is recomputed live in your browser from the locus sequence (fetched from NCBI, or the FASTA you already pulled) — a Smith–Waterman scan of all four CLsat consensuses across both strands, then merged into monomers and arrays. It is an independent re-derivation for inspection, not the pipeline's own published monomer table, so identities and boundaries can differ by a base or two from the catalogue. Use it to see structure, not as the source of record.

  • Monomer list — every monomer with its coordinates, length and % identity to the consensus.
  • Monomer map — the array drawn to genomic scale as arrows; arrow direction is monomer strand. Hover to highlight.
  • MSA — monomers stacked and aligned, with the genomic flanks attached, so substitutions that recur down the array (candidate higher-order structure) are visible as vertical stripes.

Locus ends (how the array terminates)

Shown below the monomer analysis. This answers two questions about the catalogue locus itself — anchored to its stored start/end coordinates, not to whatever fragment you fetched: (1) where does each end break — into unique flank, or straight into another satellite array (a split / composite locus)? and (2) is the terminal monomer complete or partial? There are two blocks, Left end (locus start) and Right end (locus end).

  • The verdict line is the headline. ▶ breaks into flank (with the flank class: unique / low-complexity / telomeric, and a few bases of that flank); or ▶ abuts another satellite array — naming the neighbouring locus and its family, with an [open] link to jump to it (this is the split-locus case, e.g. a CLsat1 segment running directly into a CLsat3 segment); or ■ contig / scaffold edge when the array runs off the end of the sequence. The break class comes from the precomputed flank table, so it is correct regardless of zoom.
  • The terminal monomer is the first (left end) or last (right end) monomer inside the locus interval — monomers lying in the flank or in the adjacent array are excluded. It is aligned to the chosen reference (sub-subfamily / subfamily / this-locus consensus): ref row = consensus (dim grey = positions this monomer does not cover), mono row = the genomic monomer (underline = match, red = mismatch).
  • The header reports which consensus positions the edge monomer aligns to (e.g. “aligns to consensus 1–146 of 146 bp”). This is purely descriptive coverage — it is not a “complete vs partial monomer” call. A tandem monomer has no intrinsic start, so the consensus only fixes an arbitrary reading frame; unaligned bases at an end usually just mean the edge monomer is phased differently or diverges locally, not that a fractional repeat was found. Treat the numbers as a rough coverage readout, nothing more.
  • If you have zoomed into the middle of a large locus so an end lies outside the analysed window, that block says so and points you at the Start border / End border / Full locus buttons — click one and re-run Analyze Monomers to bring the end into view.

What is solid here vs not: the break verdict (flank vs next satellite vs contig edge) is a real property of the locus boundary and is the point of this panel. The monomer-coverage numbers are only a descriptive aid — do not read them as a biological complete/partial classification.

Inspection lists

Three sets, opened from the buttons in the sidebar. In all three, clicking any row opens that locus, and partner/ortholog chips jump straight to the partner locus in another genome.

  • Orthologous loci — the 91 loci with at least one usable unique flank, scored by how many other lineages they are positionally orthologous to (flank identity ≥80%, ≥200 bp; single best hit per target assembly). 58 have at least one partner; 33 are private. In this six-assembly set the most a locus reaches is two other lineages (no flank is anchorable in more), so the presets are: has an ortholog, in both other lineages, private, and strictly private — both flanks unique yet no ortholog anywhere (2 loci). The “Orthologous to” column reads distinct assemblies, then total partner loci, because one locus can have several partner loci inside the same assembly. Positional conservation does not imply conserved content — many orthologous positions carry a different CLsat family in the two lineages.
  • Flank orthology table — the full per-locus table for all 627 catalogue loci (plus L541, which has no anchorable flank) (every assembly), with the sub-subfamily call and both flanks' cross-assembly orthology status. Filter by anchorable / has-ortholog / both-sided / same- vs different-family partner. L and R badges give each flank's class (blue = unique/anchorable, amber = low-complexity, grey = truncated, pink = satellite-adjacent, purple = telomeric); a red-bordered partner chip flags a different-family ortholog.
  • Flagged loci — 28 catalogue loci annotated by arbitration (plus the 7 uncatalogued candidates C1–C7, listed for inspection) for one of three different reasons, not all equally noteworthy: L186 is the only genuine seed/rescan contradiction (rescan favours a different family by ≥20 bits) and is the one case worth treating as an open question. The other 27 are not errors: 24 segment-boundary calls and 2 seed-conflict calls sit within a flat 15-bit margin of their runner-up family (expected near real subfamily transitions, and the margin varies widely underneath that cutoff — see Δ), plus 1 locus (L540) resolved confidently without an initial seed. Columns show the seed call and its bitscore, the strongest competitor and its bitscore, and the margin Δ between them.

Show flanks & orthologs (per locus)

In the Locus Ends & Flanks panel below the chart, this button lays out the selected locus's own flank sequence with its cross-assembly orthologs, so you can read base-by-base which flanking sequence is shared.

  • Data source: the ortholog partners, %identity, aligned length and bit scores are the precomputed ssearch36 results (the pipeline's authoritative flank-orthology table, loaded from flank_orthologs.json). The base-by-base colouring and the forward/(rc) orientation you see are recomputed live in the browser purely to draw the picture — the numbers are authoritative, the alignment rendering is a visual aid.
  • The focal locus is shown as the last 60 bp of its left flank, the collapsed satellite in the middle, and the first 60 bp of its right flank, so the array–flank junctions sit adjacent to the satellite. Each ortholog row is aligned onto that same frame (gaps as –; underline = base matches the focal locus).
  • [align] on any ortholog opens the full pairwise alignment in real genomic coordinates, with insertions and deletions shown as real gap columns rather than truncated to the 60 bp window.
  • The identity threshold selector (70 / 80 / 90%) re-filters which partners are shown; 80% is the value used throughout the paper.

Arbitration flags

FlagMeaning
UNIQUE_SEED_WITH_COMPETITORClean seed call; a competitor scores but does not threaten it.
UNIQUE_SEED_CONTRADICTIONThe rescan confidently favours a different family than the seed (Δ ≥ 20 bits). Only 1 locus (L186) — the one genuine contradiction in the catalogue.
SEED_CONFLICT_RESOLVEDSeeds from more than one subfamily fell in the locus; resolved by bitscore.
SEED_CONFLICT_RESOLVED_AMBIGUOUSAs above, but the margin is small.
SEGMENT_RESOLVEDLocus split at a sustained subfamily transition; each segment called cleanly.
SEGMENT_RESOLVED_AMBIGUOUSSplit-locus segment whose family call is within 15 bits of the runner-up family (the D3b split position itself is not in question).

Tips

  • The per-window bitscore track is the ground truth — a table label can hide a locus whose signal switches half-way.
  • If Analyze Monomers looks wrong, check you fetched the FASTA first and that the locus is zoomed in.
  • Esc closes any panel. All tables can be searched with the box at the top.
